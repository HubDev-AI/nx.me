Sources: direct App Store review pages (apps.apple.com), Apple Support community threads, Trustpilot, and secondary aggregator coverage where direct aggregator fetches were blocked (403). Google Play Store pages would not load (404) during this pass and are a gap. JustUseApp aggregator pages returned 403; its quoted fragments surfaced via web search are cited inline where used. All quotes below are reproduced verbatim as captured from those sources; a handful are truncated excerpts (the source page showed a preview only) and are marked with an ellipsis when that is the case.

Pulls covered roughly 43 distinct verbatim quotes across 9 apps (all face-rating/AI-beauty competitors). Most of the richest signal is on Apple App Store; forum quotes (looksmax.org, Apple Discussions) back up the store themes.

NXME should lead with explicit, pre-login pricing clarity, honest-trial copy ("no auto-renew ambush"), and one-tap cancel; calling out "no dark-pattern paywalls" is a differentiator that matches exactly what users are furious about.

- Implication for NXME positioning / messaging: This is the single biggest permission slip for the "identity-preserving, never a filter" wedge. Marketing line: "Your face, upgraded — not replaced." Demonstrate with before/after pairs where skin tone, ethnicity cues, and identifying features are measurably preserved. Publish a policy that NXME never lightens skin, narrows features, or Anglicizes output.

### Theme 4: Privacy anxiety — "where does my photo go?"
- Apps where it appears: FaceApp (headline case), Lensa AI
- Frequency + intensity: ~4 direct quotes but backed by widespread media coverage (FBI, Sen. Schumer). Intensity = **Medium-high** and latent for the whole category.
- Representative quotes:
  - "FaceApp filter does not explicitly tell users that photos are being sent to the cloud." — CPO Magazine summary
  - "The FBI has classified FaceApp as a counterintelligence threat because of its Russian origins" — reporting summary
  - "The viral AI avatar app Lensa undressed me—without my consent" — MIT Technology Review headline
  - "portraits including NSFW content, including avatars that look sexualized or even fully topless" (user reporting) — secondary coverage of Lensa
- Implication for NXME positioning / messaging: Concrete privacy promise up front: on-device preview where possible, photos auto-purged after N hours, US-based processing, nothing sold or used for training. "We don't send your face to Russia. We don't send it anywhere it doesn't need to go." Make this a frontloaded trust asset, not a buried policy.

- Apps where it appears: LooksMax Face Rating AI (LooxUP), Looksmaxxing, UCHAD AI, FaceScore, Umax
- Frequency + intensity: ~6 quotes across 5 face-rating apps. Intensity = **High** within the rating subcategory.
- Representative quotes:
  - "The advice is complete BS and the AI is worthless to." — LooksMax Face Rating AI, 1★, App Store, Mar 23, 2026 ("Absolutely ridiculous")
  - "gave me ratings but zero words ab appearance etc...your apps claimed functions need to work smh" — Looksmaxxing, 1★, App Store, Jan 25, 2025 ("No bueno")

- Implication for NXME positioning / messaging: "Science-backed" is necessary but not sufficient; NXME wins when it pairs measurement with a prescriptive, personalized plan. Headline benefit: "Not just a score — a 4-week plan you can actually run." Lead messaging should over-index on outputs (protocols, specific product SKUs, haircut recs that fit the user's bone structure) rather than another number.

### Theme 6: Filtered / fake / plastic output
- Apps where it appears: YouCam Makeup, Remini, FaceApp, Lensa AI
- Frequency + intensity: ~5 direct quotes. Intensity = **Medium**, but very important because it is the exact feeling NXME's "never a filter" wedge is designed to invert.
- Representative quotes:
  - "results look like wax figures, artificial" — Remini, 1★ (Trustpilot, Freddy Maldonado)
  - "most of the effects feel unrealistic and look fake" — FaceApp, 3★, Trustpilot, Nov 1, 2024
  - "the hairstyles are so fake looking and have not been updated for years" — YouCam Makeup review summary (Kimola/slashdot aggregator pull)
  - "turns photos into jokes, literally transforming people into animal characters when attempting simple edits" — YouCam Makeup review summary
  - "made me feel weird, like I was lying" — YouCam Makeup reviewer (aggregator pull)
- Implication for NXME positioning / messaging: Land the tagline "never a filter" with product proofs: no smoothing, no eye-enlarging, no jaw-narrowing. "We don't lie to your reflection." Before/after comparisons should show realistic lighting/grooming/hair changes, not AI-skin retouch.

### Theme 7: Predatory mechanics aimed at young/vulnerable users
- Apps where it appears: YouCam Makeup, LooksMax family (referral-based paywalls), Remini
- Frequency + intensity: ~3 direct quotes but a strong ethical frame in coverage. Intensity = **Medium** and rising in press coverage around teen self-esteem and face-rating apps.
- Representative quotes:
  - "serious concerns about the app's safety, warning against letting children use it as it may ruin their self-esteem and put them at risk" — YouCam Makeup review summary
  - "the app scammed their 91-year-old mother, who had no idea what she was signing up for and ended up with $40 in charges" — Remini user report (Trustpilot summary)
  - "you can't get your facial rating without either referring friends or paying money, and even after referring friends as required, the app glitches and doesn't show ratings" — Umax review summary (justuseapp aggregator)
- Implication for NXME positioning / messaging: Positions NXME to claim the adult-serious brand: "A glow-up app that respects you — no dark patterns, no shame marketing, no 'refer 3 friends to see your score.'" Useful for brand safety with parents/influencers and for winning the "grown-up" segment the incumbent category abandoned.

## Notes on methodology and trust

- Direct App Store pages (apps.apple.com) produced the bulk of the verbatim material and are quoted in full where the capture was complete.
- Where the capture was a preview/truncation on the store page (e.g., "126 characters" length cap), the quote is still verbatim up to the truncation point.
- Trustpilot and Apple Discussions quotes are verbatim from the extraction.
- Secondary coverage (MIT Technology Review, CPO Magazine, wooplus/mspoweruser aggregations, therighthairstyles.com, Kimola) was used only where a direct pull was unavailable; those quotes are marked as aggregator/coverage pulls and should be independently verified before use in marketing copy.
- Unfilled gap: Google Play Store pages would not render for this pass. A follow-up pull with a Play-scraper or scheduled WebFetch retry is the next step to harden the Android side of these themes, particularly for Umax and LooksMax AI where the Android audience skews heavier.
