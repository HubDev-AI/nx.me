# NXME TikTok Launch Simulation — Seed Document

## Product Context

NXME ("next me") is an AI-powered glow-up platform launching on TikTok. Users upload a selfie, get face analysis (468 facial landmarks, face shape classification, symmetry scoring), receive personalized style recommendations, and see realistic before/after AI-generated transformations.

Key differentiator: NXME preserves identity — it never modifies bone structure, smooths skin, or removes natural features. It recommends styling changes (hair, grooming, accessories) that work with the user's unique features.

Monetization: Free tier (1 trial + full social feed access), credit packs ($), premium subscription (~$5-10/month).

## Simulation Scenario

Simulate the first 30 days of NXME's TikTok presence. Model how different audience segments react to three content strategies:

### Strategy A: Curiosity-led ("What's my face shape?")
- Educational content about face shapes
- "Did you know you have a heart-shaped face?" style hooks
- Science angle: 468 landmarks, AI analysis
- Gradual reveal of the glow-up feature

### Strategy B: Transformation-led ("Before/after reveal")
- Immediate before/after content
- "I let AI analyze my face and this happened" hooks
- Focus on the visual transformation
- Quick path to "download the app" CTA

### Strategy C: Identity-preservation-led ("This actually looks like ME")
- Position against filter apps (FaceApp, Lensa)
- "Unlike other apps, this doesn't change your face" messaging
- Trust-building, authenticity angle
- Slower but potentially more loyal audience

## Agent Personas to Simulate

### Segment 1: Beauty enthusiasts (40% of agents)
- Age: 18-28, primarily female
- Behavior: Follow beauty influencers, try new products, share routines
- Likely response: High engagement with transformation content, may be skeptical of "not a filter" claims
- TikTok habits: Watch full tutorials, engage with comments, share to friends

### Segment 2: Self-improvement seekers (25% of agents)
- Age: 20-35, mixed gender
- Behavior: Follow personal development content, gym/fitness, dating optimization
- Likely response: Interested in the "optimize your look" angle, pragmatic
- TikTok habits: Save useful content, moderate commenting, share privately

### Segment 3: Skeptics / tech-curious (15% of agents)
- Age: 22-35, mixed gender
- Behavior: Question AI claims, compare to existing apps, look for catches
- Likely response: "Is this real?" comments, demand proof, but convert if convinced
- TikTok habits: Comment critically, watch reaction videos, share controversial takes

### Segment 4: Content creators (10% of agents)
- Age: 18-30, mixed gender
- Behavior: Looking for shareable content formats, trend-riding
- Likely response: Will try the app for content, may create UGC if results are good
- TikTok habits: Post frequently, duet/stitch, follow trends immediately

### Segment 5: Casual scrollers (10% of agents)
- Age: 16-40, mixed gender
- Behavior: Passive consumption, rarely engage, but high volume
- Likely response: Will watch but rarely comment or share
- TikTok habits: Scroll fast, skip if not hooked in 1-2 seconds

## Variables to Inject

1. **Week 1**: Launch with Strategy A/B/C (run separately to compare)
2. **Week 2**: A competitor launches a similar feature — how does sentiment shift?
3. **Week 3**: A micro-influencer (50K followers) posts a genuine reaction video
4. **Week 4**: Negative press: "AI beauty app rates your face" (mischaracterization) — how does each strategy weather it?

## Prediction Questions

1. Which strategy generates the highest simulated engagement in the first 7 days?
2. Which strategy produces the most organic UGC (content creator segment creating content about NXME)?
3. Which strategy is most resilient to the Week 4 negative press scenario?
4. What's the simulated conversion funnel: view → profile visit → app download → free trial → paid?
5. Which audience segment becomes the strongest advocate, and through which strategy?

## Success Metrics

- Engagement rate (comments + shares per view)
- Sentiment ratio (positive / negative / neutral)
- UGC generation rate (content creators making NXME content)
- Conversion intent signals ("where do I download" type comments)
- Resilience to negative narrative injection
